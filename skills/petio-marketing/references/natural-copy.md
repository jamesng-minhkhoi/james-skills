# Natural Petio Copy

Use this reference whenever a draft is described as AI-generated, boring,
over-polished, salesy, or unnatural. It is a writing and review system, not a
promise to bypass an AI detector.

## What real user posts reveal

Review public posts from ordinary owners and builders in the same audience,
not only large creator accounts. Recent examples show a few repeatable
mechanics:

- A simple rescue-dog post spread because it gave other owners an easy identity
  prompt: add your own dog and its story. The original had one person, one
  animal, one situation, and a clear emotional turn. It did not explain a
  content strategy.
- A viral animal story on X kept unfolding through small updates: a door, a
  failed attempt to solve the problem, a new surprise. Each update gave people
  a reason to return and take a side.
- A firsthand builder post about Threads mixed excitement, rough edges, an
  explicit limitation, and a direct question about willingness to pay. The
  writer admitted what was not ready instead of smoothing it into a launch
  story.
- Popular owner captions and replies often use ordinary objects, slang,
  contractions, fragments, self-deprecation, and imperfect but understandable
  wording. The humor comes from recognizing the behavior, not from adding
  marketing energy.

These are source-backed observations, not formulas. Do not copy a sentence,
creator voice, joke, image, or recognizable hook. Reuse the mechanism only
when it fits one Petio audience node and a verified owner job.

## Write from the owner, then add Petio

Use this order:

1. Pick one person, pet, object, or moment. Name what happened.
2. Add the small opinion, surprise, embarrassment, or tradeoff that makes it
   worth sharing.
3. Give the reader a way in: ask what their pet does, invite an example, or
   leave a useful observation they can recognize.
4. Add one Petio capability only if it is the natural next step. Use the
   product as a tool in the scene, not as the subject of a feature tour.
5. Add a download CTA only for an honest product bridge. Put it in a
   self-reply/comment when supported; otherwise use the documented body
   fallback. Never bolt a CTA onto unrelated entertainment.

Before the product mention, the post should pass the remove-the-brand test: if
Petio and the link disappear, the remaining moment should still be readable,
specific, and worth reacting to.

## Human signals to prefer

- first person and contractions: I, we, I've, didn't, that's;
- concrete anchors: a carrier, food bag, appointment card, camera roll,
  Tuesday night, three pets, a wet paw print;
- one uneven sentence or brief aside when it reflects the speaker;
- a real limitation: what failed, what is still being tested, or what the
  writer does not know;
- an opinion with a little stake: “I keep losing this,” “that was the part I
  got wrong,” or “my cat has a better filing system than I do”;
- an invitation with a clear answer shape: “What does yours do?”, “Which one
  would you save?”, or “Show me the weird version.”

Do not manufacture typos, vulnerability, slang, profanity, or emojis. Match the
account's existing register and use the image to carry part of the story.

## AI-smell lexicon and pattern blacklist

Treat these as rewrite triggers in the draft's own words. They are not
forbidden inside a quoted source, proper noun, technical term, URL, or exact
user-provided copy. A legitimate use can remain only when the surrounding
sentence is concrete and the word is genuinely necessary.

### High-risk words and phrases

`leverage`, `utilize`, `facilitate`, `seamless`, `robust`, `unlock`, `empower`,
`elevate`, `game-changer`, `revolutionary`, `innovative`, `transformative`,
`ecosystem`, `landscape`, `journey`, `navigate`, `foster`, `tapestry`,
`paradigm`, `synergy`, `holistic`, `actionable`, `scalable`, `streamline`,
`frictionless`, `cutting-edge`, `optimized`, `comprehensive`, `all-in-one`,
`meaningful`, `intentional`, `thoughtful`, `authentic`, `peace of mind`,
`at your fingertips`, `in today's world`, `at the end of the day`, `it's worth
noting`, `here's the thing`, `excited to share`, `proud to announce`, `sharing
a small peek`, `the quiet parts`, `the little details`, `makes everything
easier`, `built to help`, `designed to`, `meet Petio`, `that's where Petio
comes in`, `your pet deserves`, and `never miss a moment`.

### Pattern triggers

Rewrite when the draft relies on:

- “Petio is more than X. It is Y.” or “not X, but Y” as a slogan;
- “In today's fast-paced world” or another scene-free opener;
- “Here are three ways...” when the list is only there to sound structured;
- hook → generic explanation → “that's where we come in” → link;
- “Whether you are...” or “No matter if...” audience-padding;
- an abstract benefit followed by “because every pet deserves...”;
- a question that asks for comments but has no real answer shape;
- a neat three-item parallel list with no concrete decision or example;
- a polished final sentence that could advertise any app;
- repeated words such as *context*, *details*, *useful*, *small*, or *journey*
  across several posts without a new object, behavior, or story.

Prefer a plain verb, a named object, a shorter sentence, or a real example.
Do not replace every banned word with a synonym. Replace the abstraction with
what the owner actually saw or did.

## Rewrite examples

| AI-shaped draft | Human rewrite direction |
| --- | --- |
| “Petio streamlines your pet-care journey.” | Name the action: “I found the vaccination photo before leaving for the appointment.” |
| “Unlock peace of mind with seamless records.” | Name the relief and its cause: “The note was in the pet’s record, not buried in my camera roll.” |
| “Petio is more than an AI app. It is your partner in care.” | Start with the owner problem and use one proof row: “I wanted the food-label photo beside the right pet, so I could ask a better question later.” |
| “Here are three ways to make pet care easier.” | Show one scene, then invite comparison: “My dog knows the carrier sound. I still lose the appointment card. What do you keep forgetting?” |

These examples are editing patterns, not copy to reuse in a campaign.

## Mechanical preflight

Return `AI-TONE: FAIL` and rewrite if any hard trigger remains:

1. Remove the brand and CTA. If the remainder is not interesting, replace the
   concept, not just the adjectives.
2. Underline one concrete noun and one concrete action, time, number, or
   tradeoff. If none exists, return to the scene.
3. Circle every abstract benefit. Replace each with an observed behavior or
   keep only one if the evidence requires it.
4. Delete the first sentence and test whether the post becomes stronger. If it
   does, the opener was probably setup language.
5. Remove slogans, filler transitions, forced questions, unnecessary lists,
   and sentence-separator dashes. Keep a list only when the reader needs the
   count.
6. Read it aloud once. It should sound like one person telling another person
   what happened, not like a campaign brief.
7. Check the Petio proof, health-safety boundary, media source/license, and CTA
   separately. Natural language does not excuse an unsupported claim.

Mark `AI-TONE: PASS` only when the draft has a specific scene, a human point of
view, a concrete anchor, an honest reason to respond, no hard pattern trigger,
and a product mention that earns its place.

## Viral-mechanics research loop

Before using a viral example, capture:

- source URL, platform, author type, observation date, visible age and metric;
- the concrete scene and the audience identity it invites;
- the participation mechanic: add-your-own story, take a side, follow the
  unfolding situation, answer a specific question, or show a comparison;
- the emotional temperature and the visual proof;
- the Petio audience node and owner job that could honestly connect;
- what is observed, what is inferred, and what remains unknown.

Collect at least five current public examples when the task is a viral or
entertainment batch, including at least three ordinary owner accounts when the
surface makes that discoverable. Prefer the same platform, audience, and
format. Record visible metrics only; if reach or engagement is unavailable,
mark it `unknown`. A news roundup can point to examples, but it is not a
substitute for inspecting the underlying user post when the post is accessible.

Then create a Petio-native version with the same mechanic but a different
scene, words, image, and point of view. Reject it when the only bridge is a
link, the media is unlicensed, the trend is unrelated, or the post needs a
medical claim to create tension. A viral wrapper earns a test; it does not earn
an install claim or a place in the default content mix.
