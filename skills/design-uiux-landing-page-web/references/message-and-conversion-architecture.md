# Message and conversion architecture

Use this reference to map supplied landing-page content and conversion
constraints into a UIUX hierarchy before composition. It does not authorize
inventing copy, claims, proof, campaign strategy, or analytics behavior.

## Visitor-to-message fit

Inspect the relationship between traffic and page:

| Visitor condition | Message responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| Unfamiliar visitor | Explain category, audience, problem, and outcome before asking for commitment |
| Problem-aware visitor | Connect the urgent job to a specific mechanism and credible next step |
| Solution-aware visitor | Differentiate, prove fit, disclose constraints, and reduce risk |
| High-intent visitor | Make pricing, comparison, eligibility, trust, and action easy to scan |
| Returning visitor | Preserve recognition and shorten the path to the next useful action |

Do not use one generic hero for every traffic source when the visitor's intent
or promise changes materially.

## Message hierarchy

The first viewport should make these clear:

1. What is this?
2. Who is it for?
3. What valuable outcome does it create?
4. Why should the visitor believe it?
5. What can they do next?

Use an outcome-led CTA such as “Book a 20-minute assessment” or “See plans”
when the next result is known. Keep “Learn more” or “Get started” only when
the context makes the result genuinely clear.

## Claims and proof

Match proof to the claim:

| Claim | Stronger supporting evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Product capability | Real product view, demonstration, or specific workflow |
| Business result | Attributed case study, method, timeframe, and conditions |
| Trust or security | Clear policy, certification, architecture, or responsible disclosure |
| Ease or speed | Defined task, baseline, scope, and limitation |
| Customer preference | Attributed testimonial with role/context, not anonymous praise |
| Market position | Source, date, category definition, and non-misleading comparison |

Never let a logo wall, star rating, invented metric, or vague testimonial stand
in for proof that the product does not possess. Put proof near the claim it
supports and label examples, estimates, and projections honestly.

## Objections and risk

Address the highest-friction questions before commitment:

- Is this for my situation?
- What exactly do I receive?
- How much does it cost and what is excluded?
- How long does it take?
- What happens after I click or submit?
- Can I cancel, change, or recover?
- What data or permission is required?
- Is this safe, private, compliant, or appropriate for my risk level?

Do not hide cost, eligibility, material constraints, privacy effects, or
irreversible consequences behind vague CTA copy or an accordion that has no
discoverable label.

## CTA architecture

Define for each CTA:

- label and outcome;
- audience and intent;
- destination or post-click state;
- required fields and friction;
- analytics event and attribution scope;
- loading, success, error, retry, duplicate-submit, and abandonment behavior;
- visible alternative when the primary action is unavailable.

Repeat the primary CTA when the visitor reaches a natural decision point, not
after every section. Keep the label, destination, and expectation consistent.

## Section decision test

Keep a section only if it:

- clarifies the offer or audience;
- demonstrates the mechanism or benefit;
- supplies credible proof;
- answers a meaningful objection;
- explains a consequential constraint;
- enables the primary decision.

If it only adds visual texture, remove it or give it a clear compositional job.
