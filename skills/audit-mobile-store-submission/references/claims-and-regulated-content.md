# Claims and regulated content

Use this reference whenever the app touches children, family care, health,
development, finance, safety, AI, UGC, education credentials, or protected
third-party material. Do not make a legal classification from a product name.
Identify the claim, evidence, owner, and unresolved legal question.

## Claim-to-proof matrix

| Claim | In-app proof | External dependency | Audience/region | Rights or authorization | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
|  | Route/result/recording | Provider/SDK/data source |  |  |  |

Every material title, screenshot, description, onboarding statement, paywall,
notification, and reviewer note should be traceable to this matrix.

## High-risk distinctions

- **User versus subject:** an adult caregiver may use the app while a child,
  patient, student, or dependent is the subject of the recorded data.
- **Child-directed versus family-facing:** target-audience and age-rating
  decisions require product/legal evidence, not an assumption from imagery.
- **Tracking versus logging:** recording milestones or health observations can
  still create sensitive data and retention/deletion obligations.
- **Information versus medical advice:** developmental, diagnostic, treatment,
  safety, or outcome claims need substantiation and an accountable owner.
- **AI feature versus AI provider:** disclose what data leaves the device, the
  recipient, purpose, retention, user choice, and fallback behavior.
- **Licensed content versus public content:** attribution alone is not proof of
  redistribution rights.

## Audit actions

1. Extract every consequential claim from store metadata and the app.
2. Map each claim to a visible route, reproducible result, provider, or document.
3. Check that region, language, age, account tier, and feature flags do not
   make the claim true only for the developer’s environment.
4. Verify privacy, age-rating, Data Safety/App Privacy, and reviewer notes use
   the same scope and terminology.
5. Assign a legal/product owner to anything that cannot be verified locally.

Never invent licenses, clinical authority, safety guarantees, user outcomes,
testimonials, metrics, or regulatory approval. Report the missing proof as an
open gate.
