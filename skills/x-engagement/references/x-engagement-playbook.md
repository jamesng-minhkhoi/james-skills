## Conversation quality

A strong engagement action should be useful even when the reader never clicks
the account profile. Prefer:

- a direct answer before context or promotion;
- one concrete example, process detail, tradeoff, or correction;
- a question that is specific enough to answer;
- a tone that matches the parent conversation;
- visible work or a verifiable artifact when making a product claim.

Avoid empty praise, broad motivational advice, fake certainty, forced humor,
and replies that merely restate the original post.

## Post shapes

### Build proof

Use a real product change, screen recording, repository, test, or before/after.
Name the problem, what changed, and what remains unresolved.

### Test result

Show the setup, the observation, and the limitation. Do not turn a small test
into a universal result.

### Builder reaction

React to a launch, design, workflow, or failure with one clear point of view.
Keep the quoted or linked creator's result separate from your own evidence.

### Routine or story

Start with a recognizable situation, show the friction, and invite people to
share how they handle it. Mention the product only if it genuinely solves the
described problem.

### Learning note

Share a specific lesson from building or shipping. Include the context that
limits the lesson so it does not read as universal advice.

## Reply shapes

These are structures, not copy to repeat verbatim:

- **Direct answer:** “Short answer: [answer]. The tradeoff is [tradeoff].”
- **Answer plus evidence:** “[Answer]. I tested it with [setup]; the part that
  changed the result was [detail].”
- **Useful disagreement:** “I agree with [part]. I’d be careful with [part]
  because [specific reason].”
- **Curious follow-up:** “Interesting result. Did [specific variable] change
  after [event]?”
- **Relevant product fit:** “[Useful answer]. This is why I care about
  [specific workflow]; the important part is [user benefit].”
- **Requested link:** “Happy to share it: [link]. The relevant part is [why it
  helps].”

Put the answer first. Use a product mention or CTA only after relevance is
established.

## Marc Lou adapter

Use this adapter when the objective is founder-led product distribution,
launch momentum, a shipping story, or a public build-and-learn loop. The
research pattern is product-led rather than a phrase library:

- turn a real shipped product, public dataset, leaderboard, directory, free
  tool, or demo into a visible distribution surface;
- pair a short, direct founder thesis with concrete proof such as a verified
  milestone, before/after, customer result, or product artifact;
- use strong contrasts and clear opinions when the evidence and account voice
  support them;
- show the portfolio or iteration story: what shipped, what failed, what was
  learned, and what changed next;
- celebrate users and builders publicly, answer product questions briefly, and
  make the product useful before asking people to promote it;
- use launches, relaunches, integrations, rankings, and public progress as
  episodes in a continuing story rather than isolated announcements.

Marc-style directness is not permission to manufacture certainty. Verify every
revenue, user, growth, ranking, acquisition, or customer claim. A metric must
answer the criticism or claim it is attached to; traffic, return rate, or
revenue does not by itself prove that a product decision was correct. If a
person raises a UX or product concern, answer that concern directly, run or
cite an appropriate comparison, or acknowledge that the evidence is still
missing.

Do not copy Marc's recurring wording, imitate his lifestyle or revenue persona,
or assume that "just ship it" is a strategy for every product. For regulated,
health, safety, or trust-sensitive products, proof and restraint outrank
provocation.

## Tony Dinh adapter

Use this adapter when the objective is builder education, indie-founder
conversation, product discovery, or a public build-and-learn loop. Tony's
useful mechanics are not a phrase bank; they are ways to make a post easy to
understand, react to, and verify:

- name a familiar builder tension in plain language, then make the first line
  understandable without context;
- attach a concrete artifact: a working demo, short screen recording, printed
  graph, product result, launch outcome, code/workflow, or specific experiment;
- use one crisp point of view, contrast, joke, or challenge rather than a
  vague motivational paragraph;
- invite low-friction participation when it fits: show a side project, answer a
  focused question, try a demo, suggest a tool, or share an experience;
- build a continuing narrative across shipping, revenue, customer learning,
  experiments, and lessons instead of treating every post as a standalone
  announcement;
- be candid about limitations, failed experiments, audience mismatch, and the
  difference between attention and conversion.

Tony's historical examples suggest that a useful demo plus a concrete benefit
can outperform a bare announcement; a question with a clear identity or
community invitation can generate replies; and a personal milestone becomes
more discussable when it includes evidence or a real tension. These are
hypotheses to test against the account's own analytics, not guarantees.

In the available public snapshot, the pattern separates into different jobs:

- a concrete time-saving demo (“This app is going to save me so much time”)
  attracted substantial reposts and likes, and Tony later documented the demo
  as a viral post;
- identity and participation prompts such as asking what language people would
  recommend, inviting side-project self-promotion, or asking about the age at
  which people learned to code ranked strongly for replies;
- proof-backed milestones such as a GitHub history, MRR update, or product
  launch combined status, story, and evidence, producing broader attention;
- a high-reach post is not automatically a growth win: Tony's own post-viral
  analysis documented roughly 1,500 new users while questioning conversion and
  audience overlap.

Use these as role-specific hypotheses: demo posts for attention and product
discovery, questions for conversation, proof-backed stories for credibility,
and downstream analytics for conversion. Do not optimize all four jobs against
one likes or impressions leaderboard.

Do not copy Tony's wording, revenue-posting persona, or lifestyle framing. Do
not use a revenue or follower number as a substitute for user value, and do not
turn a viral joke, quote, or unrelated travel/lifestyle post into a product
strategy merely because it received reach.

## Maksim adapter

Use this adapter for mobile-app or SaaS portfolio building, distribution
experiments, acquisition funnels, and transparent founder progress. Maksim's
distinctive mechanics are operational rather than stylistic:

- make the post a field note from a real experiment: state what changed, what
  was tried, and what is still unknown;
- connect a human stake or concrete milestone to the product work, then show
  the artifact, result, or next checkpoint;
- publish funnel numbers in sequence when available: reach or clicks → store
  taps → installs → activation or revenue; name the time window and whether the
  number is measured, estimated, or projected;
- treat cross-platform content, SEO, branded search, paid acquisition, and
  app-store traffic as separate distribution cells rather than one blended
  “marketing is working” claim;
- use an honest follow-up loop: hypothesis → small test → early signal → caveat
  → next measurement. A result that is not yet conclusive can still be useful
  when the uncertainty is explicit;
- include enough implementation detail for another builder to learn, while
  keeping the post focused on one decision or result.

The public sample suggests two high-value jobs: a personal, visually legible
milestone can create broad attention, while transparent acquisition and
conversion data creates qualified builder discussion. A weekly update that
mixes MRR, content views, a launch, family life, and unfinished work can be
warm and authentic, but it is a weak causal experiment because too many
variables move at once. Split future tests when the goal is learning.

Do not copy Maksim's exact “road to” framing, revenue target, or personal
story. Do not repeat account-creation workarounds, VPN/SMS verification advice,
or other platform-bypass behavior. Treat cross-posting and AI-generated content
as experiments that need attribution and quality review, not as permission for
spam or duplicate publishing.

## Adapter selection

Use one dominant mode per draft:

| Adapter | Core pattern | Best use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Alex mechanics | spoken reaction → visible proof → useful conversation | quote posts, reactions, direct replies |
| Marc Lou mechanics | founder thesis → shipped/product proof → distribution loop | launches, milestones, build-in-public, product-led growth |
| Tony Dinh mechanics | audience tension → concrete artifact → crisp POV or participation prompt | builder education, demos, indie-founder stories, experiments |
| Maksim mechanics | real stake/milestone → distribution experiment → funnel evidence → caveat/next test | mobile apps, SaaS portfolios, acquisition and conversion learning |
| Project-native | account rules → audience language → verified artifact | domains where creator imitation would be unsafe or distracting |

Borrow mechanics, not identity. If the user's product has no verified proof or
real distribution surface, do not force a Marc-style metric or launch post;
return to a useful observation, question, or listening note.

## What to avoid

- copied hooks, recurring interjections, or recognizable creator phrasing;
- “AI will fix everything,” generic growth claims, and manufactured urgency;
- unsupported revenue, download, rating, retention, or conversion claims;
- unrelated replies containing a product link;
- the same screenshot, CTA, or nearly identical reply repeated across targets;
- diagnosing or fear-based pet-health hooks;
- treating one high-impression post as proof of product-market fit.
- using unrelated metrics to win an argument about a product flaw;
- turning a leaderboard, revenue screenshot, or launch result into a promise
  that another product will achieve the same outcome.

## Winning-post diagnostic

Use this when the user asks what drives engagement, attention, or “what works”
for an account. The goal is to discover conditional patterns, not to reward
outliers or copy a creator's surface style.

For the row-level capture contract, media inspection, reply/repost coding, and
source-confidence fields, load [the content-audit schema](x-content-audit-schema.md).
For CSV/JSON samples, use `scripts/audit_x_sample.py` to calculate descriptive
normalized rates and cluster summaries before interpreting the results.

### Define the outcome before scoring

Separate three layers:

| Layer | Signals | What it can support |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Attention | impressions/views, profile visits, detail expands, video completion | the post earned a chance to be noticed |
| Conversation | qualified replies, reposts with commentary, bookmarks, relevant follows | the idea created participation or utility |
| Conversion | link clicks, signups, demos started, installs, activated users, sales | the attention reached the intended business outcome |

Likes are context. They are not a proxy for conversion, product quality, or
audience fit. A post with high reach and low qualified response may be useful
for awareness but weak for demand. A post with modest reach and strong replies
or clicks may be a better model for a small product account.

### Build a comparable sample

Collect at least 8–12 apparent winners and 8–12 weak or ordinary posts where
possible. Record account, date/age, original/reply/quote/repost, topic, first
line, media/artifact, POV, question/CTA, impressions, replies, reposts, likes,
bookmarks, profile visits, clicks, and downstream outcome. Keep replies,
original posts, and reposts in separate groups; do not compare a fresh reply
with a month-old original as if they were the same unit.

Prefer rates over counts when data is available:

```text
engagement rate = (likes + replies + reposts + bookmarks) / impressions
qualified-response rate = (relevant replies + qualified clicks) / impressions
conversion rate = desired downstream events / qualified clicks or visits
```

If impressions are missing, use follower count only as a rough denominator and
label the result `confounded`. Public mirrors and analytics sites can be stale,
use different metric ordering, or expose only selected posts. Verify the source
and timestamp before using the number.

### Code the post, then compare clusters

For every post, code these fields before looking for a formula:

| Field | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| Audience job | learn, laugh, compare, be seen, get feedback, discover a tool |
| Hook tension | time saved, surprising result, status challenge, fear, curiosity, identity |
| Proof | working demo, screenshot/video, number, story, third-party result, none |
| Participation | specific question, reply-to-try, self-promotion, disagreement, none |
| Emotional shape | delight, surprise, pride, frustration, humor, aspiration, controversy |
| Product distance | directly useful, adjacent, personal, unrelated |
| Experiment surface | product, SEO, social video, paid ads, app store, personal story, none |
| Evidence status | measured, estimated, projected, anecdotal, unknown |

Then compare winners against the baseline. Report a driver only when it recurs
across multiple posts and improves the requested outcome, not merely because it
appears in the single highest-reach post. Also report non-drivers and
confounders: topic novelty, a large account mention, news timing, age, paid
distribution, media format, or an unusually active reply thread.

### Use a winning formula as a hypothesis

Write the result in this form:

```text
For [audience] trying to [job], [hook tension] + [specific artifact/proof]
+ [one clear POV or useful contrast] + [low-friction participation prompt]
is associated with [metric] in [sample], while [distraction pattern] tends to
produce [weak or unqualified outcome].
```

For a Tony Dinh-style builder sample, a likely starting hypothesis is:

```text
recognizable builder tension → concrete working artifact or result → crisp
POV/joke/contrast → easy way for builders to respond, try, or compare
```

Treat “likely” as a prompt for an experiment. It must be replaced with the
account's own sample size, metrics, dates, and evidence before being called a
winning formula.

For a Maksim-style mobile-app or SaaS sample, a likely starting hypothesis is:

```text
real personal or product milestone → one distribution experiment → funnel
numbers in order → explicit caveat → next measurement
```

This is especially useful for conversion learning because it makes the gap
between attention and installs visible. Do not combine SEO clicks, social
views, store taps, installs, and projected revenue into one rate unless their
denominators and attribution windows genuinely match.

### Detect distractions explicitly

Label a post `distraction candidate` when it is off-audience, has no useful
artifact or point of view, and does not support the current objective. Common
examples include generic motivation, vague AI hype, lifestyle/travel updates,
unrelated celebrity or news reactions, product announcements with no user
problem or proof, and links that ask for attention without explaining the
payoff.

Do not automatically delete or ban these posts. They may serve relationship,
personality, or reach goals. Instead, keep them out of the product-growth
formula unless they outperform on qualified replies, profile visits, or
conversion. A high-view distraction can be an attention spike, not a strategy.

### Output format

Return:

1. the sample window and source confidence;
2. winner/ordinary/distraction counts by format and topic;
3. repeated positive drivers with supporting examples;
4. repeated weak patterns and confounders;
5. the provisional formula and what it does **not** prove;
6. one or two controlled tests with primary metric, guardrails, and stop rule.

## Experiment design

Start with a small, distinct batch rather than maximizing volume. A useful
founder experiment can compare:

| Cell | Example | Record |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Build proof | Real screenshot or short demo | qualified replies, profile visits, clicks |
| Conversation | Useful replies without CTA | reply quality, follows, profile visits |
| CTA placement | Relevant self-reply vs. no CTA | clicks and downstream sessions |
| Story | Routine/friction post | target-audience replies and follows |
| Product loop | Useful free surface, dataset, leaderboard, or demo | qualified users, shares, repeat visits, activation |
| Media role | Proof screenshot vs. demo vs. human/lifestyle image | attention, qualified replies, profile visits |
| Hashtag/mention | No hashtag vs. one relevant tag; peer mention vs. none | reach quality, replies, profile visits |
| Reply depth | Acknowledgement vs. answer/evidence/question | conversation quality, follow-up replies |
| Repost role | Endorsement vs. commentary vs. community support | relevant reach and follower quality |

Change one major variable at a time when possible. Record the audience, target
post reach, topic, asset, and timing before comparing results.
