# X Engagement

Use X as a conversation and learning surface, not a slot machine. Make each
post or reply useful to the person reading the original conversation, then
measure qualified attention rather than chasing likes.

Load [the engagement playbook](references/x-engagement-playbook.md) for voice,
post/reply shapes, target selection, and account adapters. Load [policy and
measurement](references/x-policy-and-measurement.md) before live execution or
when interpreting performance.

## Operating modes

Choose one mode before doing work:

- **Draft:** produce copy, media direction, target rationale, CTA options, and
  a preflight report. Do not publish.
- **Research and queue:** find relevant conversations, rank them by fit and
  usefulness, and prepare a small reply queue. Do not publish.
- **Live execution:** only when the user explicitly authorizes posting,
  replying, liking, following, or other account actions. Verify every visible
  result afterward.
- **Listening:** monitor topics, launches, customer language, or risk signals
  without posting. Use this when an account or channel is intentionally
  dormant.
- **Performance audit:** compare winning and weak posts from an account or
  project. Use this to identify repeatable attention and conversation drivers,
  separate vanity virality from qualified response, and label distracting
  formats before copying them.

## Non-negotiable gates

1. **Account and voice:** identify the account, audience, active lane, and
   project-specific voice rules. Do not apply a founder voice to a product
   account or one project to another without an explicit adapter.
2. **Proof:** verify every number, result, testimonial, screenshot claim,
   product capability, and link destination. Label observation, inference, and
   hypothesis separately.
3. **Relevance:** a reply must answer, extend, clarify, or respectfully question
   the original post. Never use an unrelated conversation as an ad slot.
4. **Safety:** do not diagnose, promise health outcomes, invent authority, or
   create urgency. Read the project's claims/safety guidance for sensitive
   domains.
5. **Authenticity:** no copied creator wording, fake persona, engagement
   exchange, follow churn, mass duplicate replies, indiscriminate follows,
   irrelevant hashtags, or link-only repetition.
6. **Authorization:** drafting is the default. Live actions require explicit
   user authorization for the current batch and account.

## Workflow

### 1. Build the account brief

Before drafting, record:

- account and owner; product, founder, or listening lane;
- target audience and the conversation spaces they actually use;
- objective: useful conversation, qualified profile visits, launch awareness,
  product discovery, traffic, installs, or learning;
- one primary metric and two guardrail metrics;
- available proof, media, links, and constraints;
- whether the request is draft, queue, live execution, or listening.

If the workspace contains project guidance, read it before writing. For the
Petio workspace, look for `index.md`, `CLAUDE.md`, `platforms/x.md`,
`plans/2026-08-22-alex-style-adaptation.md`, `proof/proof-ledger.md`, and
`voice/claims-and-safety.md`. The `@JamesNMK` founder adapter is mandatory for
X posts and replies; the Petio product account remains dormant unless the user
explicitly reopens it. If more than one creator reference is available, choose
one dominant adapter for the draft and record it. Do not blend recognizable
creator voices into a generic imitation.

### 2. Research and select the conversation

Search by the user's problem, audience language, product category, launch
event, or creator conversation—not only by the brand name. For each candidate,
record:

| Candidate | Topic fit | What can be added | Account/audience fit | Risk | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
|  | high/medium/low | answer, example, tradeoff, question |  |  | reply/skip/listen |

Prefer a smaller set of distinct, recent, relevant conversations over a large
volume target. Skip posts where the only contribution would be a pitch, a
generic compliment, or a recycled reply. Do not use technical X limits as a
posting quota.

### 3. Choose the smallest useful format

- **Reply:** answer the exact question first; add one concrete detail,
  tradeoff, example, or next step; ask one focused follow-up only when it
  genuinely advances the conversation.
- **Original post:** one reaction or observation, one concrete artifact or
  proof item, one point of view, and an optional contextual CTA.
- **Quote post:** make the added commentary useful without implying that the
  quoted result belongs to the account or product.
- **Thread:** use only when a single post cannot carry the explanation. Give
  each post a job; remove filler, repeated hooks, and manufactured suspense.
- **Self-reply:** use for supporting detail, a relevant link, or a demo after
  the main post earns context. Do not turn every self-reply into a funnel.
- **Listening note:** capture the exact language, source, date, confidence,
  and possible follow-up; do not convert a signal into a claim automatically.

### 4. Draft with evidence and human voice

Use the selected account adapter, then add a concise spoken hook, concrete
detail, visible work, and a clear point of view. Keep the language natural,
specific, and easy to respond to. Match the emotional temperature of the
parent post. For replies, put the useful answer before any account mention or
link.

Use a CTA only when it is the natural next step. Prefer a contextual
self-reply or an answer to a direct request over attaching the same link to
every post. If a link is included, say why the reader should open it and verify
the destination.

### 5. Run the preflight

Check:

- Is the first line clear without the media?
- Does the media prove the claim rather than decorate it?
- Is every number, product capability, quote, and outcome sourced?
- Does each metric actually support the claim being made, rather than merely
  showing that the product is popular or growing?
- Does the reply make sense if the reader never visits the profile?
- Is the CTA relevant, optional, and non-manipulative?
- Is the wording original rather than a creator imitation?
- Does it avoid health, legal, financial, privacy, or safety overclaiming?
- Does it avoid bulk, duplication, irrelevant hashtags, engagement exchange,
  follow churn, and link-only behavior?

Return `ready`, `needs evidence`, `needs rewrite`, or `do not post`, with the
reason and the exact missing evidence.

### 6. Execute only when authorized

For live work, publish or engage through the available authenticated X surface.
Do not claim success from a tool/API response alone. Immediately verify the
visible account, exact text, media, link, parent post, and public permalink.
Record failures, partial publication, deleted content, rate limits, warnings,
or account-state changes. Stop the batch if replies are being marked as spam,
the account enters a challenge, or the target conversation changes materially.

Never silently like, follow, repost, quote, DM, or publish beyond the action
the user authorized.

### 7. Measure and learn

Create an execution record with the post/reply URL, timestamp, account, target
post, topic, format, media, CTA placement, proof source, and experiment cell.
Capture a stable snapshot after 24–72 hours, and later when the experiment
needs more time. Separate organic and promoted data when applicable.

Evaluate the primary objective first. Use likes and impressions as context,
not as proof of product value. For founder experiments, compare topic fit,
asset type, CTA placement, qualified replies, profile visits, link clicks, and
downstream events. Record what changed before declaring a winning format.

For a performance audit, follow the [winning-post diagnostic](references/x-engagement-playbook.md#winning-post-diagnostic). Do not infer a formula from
one viral post or from raw likes alone: sample winners and weak posts from a
comparable window, normalize by impressions when available, and distinguish
attention, conversation, and conversion outcomes. If impressions or account
analytics are unavailable, mark the conclusion as confounded rather than
pretending that public counts are comparable.

For the capture fields and media/reply/repost/hashtag coding rules, load
[the content-audit schema](references/x-content-audit-schema.md). When a CSV or
JSON sample is available, run
`python3 scripts/audit_x_sample.py --input <sample.csv> --output <report.md>`
from this skill directory, then interpret the report using the diagnostic.
The script is descriptive only: it must never fill missing metrics, infer
causality, or publish account activity.
