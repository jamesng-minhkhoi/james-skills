# Natural Threads voice adapters

Use these as mechanics, not as a phrase bank. They are project-agnostic and
must be adapted to the account, audience, proof, and parent conversation.

## The shared register

Make the post sound like a person noticed something and decided to say it, not
like a content system completed a brief.

- Start from a real moment, decision, surprise, mistake, artifact, or tradeoff.
- Use the account's spoken vocabulary, contractions, and sentence length.
- Keep one main thought. A short post can be unfinished; it does not need a
  summary paragraph.
- Use one concrete detail: an object, behavior, time, number, screen, failure,
  customer phrase, or before/after.
- Let the image or video prove part of the point instead of narrating it all.
- End with a useful observation or one easy question. Do not append a CTA by
  reflex.

## Named creator references

Some workspaces may contain a named creator adaptation, including the existing
Alex-style and indie-builder guidance in the X engagement playbook. Use that
material only to extract mechanics: spoken reaction, visible proof, casual
register, a clear point of view, and a continuing build/test story. Never copy
phrases, recurring hooks, biography, lifestyle, relationship, or persona. If a
named reference conflicts with the account's own evidence or voice, use the
project-native adapter instead.

## Adapter: spoken builder observation

Use for a founder or builder sharing a product moment, design decision, or
small discovery:

```text
spoken reaction or noticing → precise consequence → visible work or question
```

Useful prompts:

- What did I notice that I did not expect?
- What looked small but changed the decision?
- What is the oddly specific consequence another builder or user will
  recognize?
- What can the attached screenshot, demo, or artifact prove without prose?

Keep the hedge when it is honest: “I think,” “the weird part is,” “I expected X,
but,” or “this is probably obvious, but.” Do not manufacture vulnerability or
copy a named creator's recurring opener.

## Adapter: build-in-public field note

Use when there is real shipped work or a real test:

```text
what changed → what surprised/broke → what is next
```

Name the limitation. A small result with a caveat is more credible than a
smooth launch announcement. Keep metrics verified and time-bounded. Avoid
“excited to announce,” “proud to share,” and a feature list with no decision or
lesson.

## Adapter: indie-builder education

Use when the audience is builders or operators:

```text
familiar builder tension → concrete artifact/example → crisp POV → optional
low-friction invitation
```

The invitation can be “what did you try?”, “show me yours,” or “is this how you
handle it?” only when a real exchange is possible. Do not optimize every post
for replies; a useful artifact may be the whole post.

## Adapter: project-native conversation

Use for product, community, or personal accounts that should not sound like a
founder. Match the audience's current language and emotional temperature. If a
product is mentioned, disclose it naturally and put the useful observation
before the mention. Never fake a founder persona to borrow builder credibility.

## Anti-AI-text preflight

Reject or rewrite copy that has several of these symptoms:

- generic openers such as “some days are all admin” or “in today's world”;
- abstract warmth such as “the quiet parts,” “the little details,” or “makes
  everything easier” without a specific moment;
- a clean marketing bridge such as “sharing a small peek at…”;
- a neat hook → explanation → CTA structure with no human irregularity;
- repeated em-dashes, stacked three-part lists, slogan endings, or “not X but
  Y” contrasts used for rhythm rather than meaning;
- “game-changer,” “seamless,” “unlock,” “empower,” “journey,” “revolutionary,”
  “peace of mind,” or similar category language;
- a question that exists only to force comments;
- a product name or link that arrives before the reader has received value;
- polished wording that could be pasted onto any project's account.

### Mechanical banned-word and punctuation gate

Apply these checks to the draft's own words, not quoted parent text, proper
nouns, brand names, technical terms, identifiers, URLs, or user-requested
exact copy:

- AI-TONE-01: rewrite delve, leverage, utilize, facilitate, seamless, robust,
  unlock, empower, elevate, game-changer, revolutionary, innovative,
  transformative, ecosystem, landscape, journey, navigate, foster, tapestry,
  paradigm, synergy, holistic, actionable, scalable, streamline, frictionless,
  cutting-edge, at the end of the day, in today's world, it's worth noting,
  here's the thing, excited to share, a testament to, small but mighty,
  sharing a small peek, the quiet parts, the little details, and makes
  everything easier;
- AI-TONE-02: do not use em dashes (—) or en dashes (–) as sentence
  punctuation. ASCII hyphens (-) are for legitimate compounds, identifiers,
  versions, URLs, or code only, never sentence separators;
- AI-TONE-03: rewrite generic bridges, "not X but Y" templates, slogan
  endings, empty questions, and polished CTAs that could fit any project;
- AI-TONE-04: rewrite unnecessary three-part lists, mirrored clauses, or
  repeated parallel phrasing used mainly to sound polished.

Return AI-TONE: PASS only after the concrete-detail and voice-note checks also
pass. On failure, return AI-TONE: FAIL, the rule ID, and the exact phrase to
rewrite. This reduces templated signals; it cannot guarantee how a platform or
detector will classify text.

Run the voice-note test: read the draft aloud once. If it sounds like a
landing-page caption, rewrite from the concrete event. Remove the product name
and CTA temporarily; if the remaining observation is not interesting on its
own, do not post it yet.

## Before/after diagnostic

Synthetic pattern:

> Some days are all admin. I like the quiet parts of the work—the little
> details that make everything easier. Sharing a small product peek.

Natural builder pattern:

> I thought the hard part would be the upload screen. It was deciding what to
> show first once there were three real examples in front of me.
>
> The screenshot is the current answer. I expect it to change again.

The second version works because it contains a decision, a concrete artifact,
and an honest next step. It does not try to sound casual by adding emojis or
pretend uncertainty.

## Voice selection and learning

Record the selected adapter, account type, topic, proof/artifact, CTA placement,
and human feedback in the learning ledger. Treat comments such as “sounds like
AI,” “too polished,” “too salesy,” or “this feels like me” as feedback signals,
not as proof of a detector's classification. Look for repeated feedback across
comparable posts, then promote only the underlying mechanic—not a copied phrase—
into shared guidance.
